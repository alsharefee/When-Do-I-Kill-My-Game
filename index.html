Frequently Asked Questions

Steam Wishlists & Indie Game Revenue FAQ

Answers to common questions about Steam algorithm mechanics, wishlist conversion rates, Valve platform cuts, and project runway calculations.

How many Steam wishlists do I need before launching?

Most indie industry data analysts (including Simon Carless of GameDiscoverCo and Chris Zukowski of How To Market A Game) suggest having at least 5,000 to 10,000 wishlists before launching.

Reaching this threshold significantly increases your likelihood of appearing on Steam's organic Popular Upcoming carousel during the week prior to launch, which in turn fuels high initial sales velocity and pushes your game into the New and Trending section. Games releasing with fewer than 1,000 wishlists often get swallowed by the hundreds of daily Steam releases.

What is the average Steam wishlist to sales conversion rate?

On average, indie games convert between 5% and 20% of their total wishlist count into unit sales during the first week of launch.

Industry baseline medians hover around 7–10%, healthy anticipated releases hit 10–15%, while breakout genre hits often exceed 20%. Factors that increase conversion include high demo playtime, positive review velocity, localized translations, and strong genre demand (e.g. Roguelikes, Simulation, and Deckbuilders historically convert higher than narrative puzzle games).

How much money do developers actually keep from Steam sales?

A good rule of thumb is that developers take home roughly 50% to 55% of gross revenue (often called the Golden Ratio or Gaben's Toll).

Here is where the gross revenue goes:

  • Valve Cut: 30% platform distribution fee.
  • Regional Pricing: Average 10%–15% discount impact across international markets.
  • Refunds: Typical Steam return rates are 5% to 8%.
  • VAT & Taxes: Varies by country and withholding agreements.
How do first-week sales predict Year 1 and lifetime revenue?

According to historical data from the Boxleiter method and multiple indie publisher studies:

  • Year 1 Net Revenue: Approximately 3.0x your Week 1 revenue.
  • Lifetime (5-Year) Revenue: Approximately 7.5x your Week 1 revenue.

The long tail is sustained by participating in Steam seasonal sales (Summer, Autumn, Winter, Spring), genre-specific festivals, bundle sales, and major content updates.

How many wishlists does Steam Next Fest give you?

Steam Next Fest is a slot machine heavily influenced by your pre-festival momentum and demo engagement:

If you enter with fewer than 1,000 wishlists, the median gain is roughly 300 to 700 wishlists. If you enter with 2,000+ wishlists and have a polished, streamable demo, you can gain 1,500 to 5,000+ wishlists during the festival week as Steam features games with high active demo player counts on festival category pages.

When should an indie developer cancel or kill their project?

Cancelling a project is painful, but continuing on a commercially hopeless trajectory consumes irreplaceable time and savings. Red flags include:

  • Your wishlist velocity is flat (< 5–10 wishlists/day) despite public devlogs, trailers, and social outreach.
  • Your personal savings runway has shrunk to under 3 months without an outside publisher or contract income.
  • Your projected Year 1 net revenue cannot cover a fraction of the debt or lost opportunity cost incurred during development.

In these scenarios, cutting scope to release a tight vertical slice or pivoting to a fresh prototype with proven market hook is often the healthiest professional move.

Methodology & Assumptions

Gaben's Toll (55% Ratio): Accounts for Valve's 30% cut, taxes, regional pricing (where your game is discounted in emerging markets), and standard refund rates based on empirical analytics from Chris Zukowski (How To Market A Game) and Simon Carless (GameDiscoverCo).

Year 1 & Lifetime Tails: Standard industry estimates based on the Boxleiter method and Valve partner data. Year 1 sales are roughly 3x your Week 1 sales. Lifetime 5-year sales typically equal approximately 7.5x launch week.

Steam Next Fest Projection: Modeled as a progressive curve based on historical Next Fest developer survey data. Baseline discovery yields ~250–600 wishlists for smaller indies, scaling smoothly as higher pre-existing wishlists push your demo into popular festival category queues (~1.5k at 2k wishlists, ~5k+ at 10k wishlists).

The Starving Artist Model: Assumes you still need to pay rent. As a dev, working on this game actively burns a hole in your wallet.
